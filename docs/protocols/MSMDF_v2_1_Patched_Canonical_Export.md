# ENGLISH REVOLUTION, CIVIL WAR & GLORIOUS REVOLUTION
## MAP Narrative Note — Canonical Narrative Representation

The [[English Revolution]] emerged from centuries of constitutional tension between monarchy and [[Parliament]].

Beginning with:
━━━━━━━━━━
1215 — [[Magna Carta]]
━━━━━━━━━━

the principle gradually developed that:
### the monarch was subject to law.

Under the [[Stuart Dynasty]], especially:
- [[James I]]
- [[Charles I]]

the doctrine of:
# [[Divine Right Theory]]

intensified conflict with Parliament.

Political grievances such as:
- arbitrary taxation,
- forced loans,
- and [[Ship Money]]

Retrieval Anchor:
### [[Ship Money]] → arbitrary taxation without parliamentary consent

combined with religious tensions involving:
- [[Puritans]]
- and [[William Laud]]

to create revolutionary instability.

The:
# [[Eleven Years’ Tyranny]]

deepened fears of absolutism.

The:
# [[Bishops’ Wars]]

forced the recall of Parliament and accelerated constitutional crisis.

Conflict escalated through:
- [[Short Parliament]]
- [[Long Parliament]]
- and [[Grand Remonstrance]]

until England descended into:
# [[English Civil War]]

between:
- [[Cavaliers]]
and
- [[Roundheads]].

The:
# [[New Model Army]]

under [[Oliver Cromwell]] transformed the parliamentary war effort.

━━━━━━━━━━
1645 — [[Battle of Naseby]]
━━━━━━━━━━

became the decisive parliamentary victory.

The Revolution radicalized through:
# [[Pride’s Purge]]

and culminated in:
# 1649 — Execution of [[Charles I]]

which challenged the sacred foundations of monarchy.

England briefly became a:
# [[Commonwealth]]

under the Protectorate system.

# [[Instrument of Government]] (1653)

became England’s first written constitution.

Following:
# [[Restoration]] (1660)

constitutional tensions survived beneath political stability.

The:
# [[Habeas Corpus Act]]

strengthened:
### [[Rule of Law]]

Retrieval Anchor:
### [[Habeas Corpus Act]] → safeguard against arbitrary imprisonment

while the:
# [[Exclusion Crisis]]

generated early party politics through:
- [[Whigs]]
- and [[Tories]].

Under:
# [[James II]]

fear of Catholic absolutism intensified sharply.

Events such as:
- [[Declaration of Indulgence]]
- and [[Seven Bishops Case]]

deepened distrust toward monarchy.

This culminated in:
# [[Glorious Revolution]] (1688)

when:
# [[William of Orange]]

intervened and [[James II]] fled.

The:
# [[Convention Parliament]]

invited:
- [[William III]]
- and [[Mary II]]

to rule jointly.

The:
# [[Bill of Rights]] (1689)

permanently restricted monarchy and strengthened parliamentary supremacy.

England completed its constitutional transition:

### from Absolutist Monarchy
➡️
### to [[Constitutional Monarchy]] under Parliamentary Supremacy

Long-term consequences included:
- growth of [[Rule of Law]]
- development of constitutional government
- rise of parliamentary sovereignty
- and foundations of modern democratic constitutionalism.
